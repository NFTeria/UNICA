The problem

The till and the treasury are two places

A merchant accepting crypto either holds the asset and takes the risk, or converts immediately and pays a spread to do it. The gap between the two is where the money goes.

The quote

The merchant names an exact amount

Recipient, payout asset, exact amount, deadline — registered by an authenticated call. The payer supplies only an order id, so nothing a caller sends can change who gets paid.

The swap

The payer brings whatever they hold

The swap runs through Uniswap's official Universal Router into a v4 pool whose hook admits only this path.

The enforcement

Exact fill, or nobody moves

The hook checks the realised output against the order inside the swap. Short fill reverts the whole transaction. It holds no custody, takes no fee, returns no delta and runs no oracle — and its permission bits are readable from its address alone, with no RPC call and no trust in us.

The proof

A receipt, and somewhere to read it

One receipt per settlement, carrying the order id, emitted inside the swap. A subgraph indexes it, so the merchant's record of what settled is a query rather than a promise.

The identity

A merchant is a name, and a delegate is not the owner

The merchant holds an ENSv2 name on Sepolia and never transfers it. A delegate is authorised for one text record key — not the name, not the payment address, and not the ability to pass that authority to anyone else.
